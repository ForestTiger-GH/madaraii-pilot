# Research Agenda — WORK-0001

Status: admitted Research program, amended by `INPUT-0002`  
Qualification Baseline: `INPUT-0001` plus supplemental evidence requirement `INPUT-0002`.  
Primary consumer: professional Russian bank user evaluating corporate-borrower environment, corporate-credit implications, and AПК-specific transmission.  
Primary time window: Q2 2026, H1 2026 context, 2025 causal/comparative base.

## Materiality and stopping rule

A Research question is material when its answer can change at least one of: understanding of borrower revenue/cost/margin/cash-flow dynamics; working-capital or investment financing need; debt-service capacity; credit demand; credit availability/pricing; sector/firm risk differentiation; or the interpretation of AПК exposures.

Research stops when additional search is unlikely to change the identified mechanism, its direction/material heterogeneity, competing explanations, confidence, bank relevance, or the bounded outlook materially. The program does not seek exhaustive macro coverage.

## Qualified Topics

### TOPIC-01 — From 2025 overheating to H1 2026 cooling: real activity, demand, prices and capacity

**Problem.** A bank needs to know whether Q2 2026 represents broad weakening, controlled normalization, or a heterogeneous regime shift, and which demand/supply channels drive it.

**Questions.** What materially changed in output and demand by Q2? Which parts reflect cooling of domestic demand versus supply/capacity constraints, labor conditions, inflation and relative-price effects? How do 2025 conditions explain H1 2026? Which mechanisms are persistent or temporary?

**Scope.** GDP/activity composition, household/corporate demand, industrial/services construction where material, inflation and producer/consumer pricing, labor/wages/capacity, business expectations. Earlier history only to identify regime. Fiscal/external details only as interfaces to TOPIC-04.

**Non-goals.** Comprehensive national-accounts digest; full household analysis; sector encyclopedia.

**Evidence strategy.** Rosstat primary releases; Bank of Russia monetary-policy/macro publications and enterprise/regional monitoring; selected high-quality secondary interpretation only for challenge or inaccessible detail.

**Competing accounts/challenges.** Demand cooling versus supply ceiling; statistical base effects; nominal growth masking real weakness; aggregate growth masking sector divergence.

**Stopping.** Sufficient evidence to characterize the Q2 regime and transmission-relevant heterogeneity, with confidence and open uncertainties.

### TOPIC-02 — Monetary and financial conditions as a corporate-finance transmission mechanism

**Problem.** High rates can affect firms through debt repricing, investment hurdle rates, liquidity preferences, working capital and interest coverage even when headline corporate credit still grows.

**Questions.** How did monetary conditions evolve through Q2 2026? How did policy/market rates and expectations translate into corporate borrowing cost and availability? What does this imply for interest expense, refinancing, cash/liquidity behavior, investment and working-capital choices? Which firms are most exposed by debt structure and margins?

**Scope.** Key-rate path and guidance; money/credit conditions; corporate loan/deposit rates; yield/financing conditions; debt-service burden evidence; refinancing and liquidity channels; monetary transmission lags.

**Non-goals.** Bank profitability/NIM analysis; institution-specific portfolio modeling.

**Evidence strategy.** Bank of Russia policy decisions, monetary-policy report, monetary conditions/transmission, financial-stability and corporate-sector materials, official rate/credit statistics; academic/practitioner evidence only where needed to explain mechanisms.

**Challenges.** Average rates versus marginal borrower rates; fixed/floating/repricing heterogeneity; nominal balance-sheet growth; subsidized lending; selection effects as weak borrowers exit new lending.

**Stopping.** Corporate-finance transmission can be explained without treating credit growth as a sufficient proxy for financial ease.

### TOPIC-03 — Corporate financial condition and heterogeneity: margins, cash flow, working capital and investment

**Problem.** Macro aggregates must be translated into firm economics. Q2 accounting data are incomplete/lagged, so evidence must distinguish direct observations from proxies.

**Questions.** What evidence exists on corporate profit dynamics, margins/profitability, costs, wage pressure, inventories/receivables/payables, payment stress, investment and business expectations? Which sectors/business models diverge and why? Where can weaker operations coexist with resilient debt service, or vice versa?

**Scope.** Rosstat corporate financial statistics, investment, inventories/turnover where available; Bank of Russia enterprise surveys/regional economy; selected sector/corporate disclosures as triangulation when materially informative.

**Non-goals.** Firm-by-firm credit analysis; unsupported estimation of an aggregate interest-coverage ratio.

**Evidence strategy.** Primary corporate/official data first; survey and sector sources for timely Q2 direction; explicit temporal labels and proxy status.

**Challenges.** Publication lags, survivorship, inflation in nominal profits, sector concentration, exports/public demand, large-versus-SME differences.

**Stopping.** Enough evidence to describe the main corporate balance-sheet/cash-flow tensions and heterogeneity that changes credit interpretation.

### TOPIC-04 — Fiscal, external and exchange-rate channels that materially alter corporate outcomes

**Problem.** In Russia, public demand, oil/export revenue, trade restrictions, exchange-rate movement and import conditions can offset or amplify monetary cooling and create sharp sector splits.

**Questions.** Which fiscal/external/ruble developments were material in H1/Q2 2026? Through what mechanisms did they affect domestic demand, exporters, importers, capex/input costs, working capital, cash flow and financing needs? Which effects are transitory versus regime-relevant?

**Scope.** Federal budget stance/major expenditure and revenue dynamics where macro-relevant; oil/export and balance-of-payments conditions; ruble/exchange-rate channel; import/logistics/trade restrictions only to the depth needed for corporate finance.

**Non-goals.** Geopolitical chronology; full fiscal-accounting review; commodity-market encyclopedia.

**Evidence strategy.** Ministry of Finance/Federal Treasury/Rosstat/Bank of Russia primary data; official energy/customs/trade sources where available; credible market sources for price/logistics context with explicit role.

**Challenges.** Timing/cash versus accrual; seasonal budget execution; exchange-rate causality; oil-price versus volume/sanctions effects; public-sector demand concentration.

**Stopping.** Sufficient to identify material offsets/amplifiers to the domestic cooling narrative and affected borrower groups.

### TOPIC-05 — Corporate credit market: demand, supply, composition and emerging credit-risk signals

**Problem.** The bank needs an interpretation of credit growth that separates borrower financing need from pricing, standards, policy support, working-capital demand, investment, refinancing and lender risk appetite.

**Questions.** How did corporate lending evolve through Q2 2026? What evidence indicates demand versus supply drivers? How did rates, maturity, sector mix, preferential lending and refinancing shape growth? What early credit-quality/restructuring/overdue indicators matter for debt-service interpretation?

**Scope.** Banking-sector corporate loan dynamics, ruble/FX, short/long where available, lending conditions, borrower demand, quality/overdue/restructuring signals, related corporate deposits/liquidity when material.

**Non-goals.** Individual-bank competitive analysis; regulatory-capital forecasting.

**Evidence strategy.** Bank of Russia banking-sector development reviews, credit/monetary statistics, lending surveys/enterprise monitoring, financial-stability material.

**Challenges.** Currency revaluation, large one-off borrowers, subsidized segments, refinancing counted as lending, portfolio growth despite tighter marginal conditions.

**Stopping.** Enough to state what corporate credit behavior does and does not imply about borrower health and financing demand.

### TOPIC-06 — AПК transmission model: production, prices, margins, seasonality, support and credit

**Problem.** AПК combines biological/seasonal production, commodity prices, weather/harvest uncertainty, working-capital peaks, export channels and state-supported finance; these can materially break the general corporate transmission model.

**Questions.** What materially shaped AПК economics in H1/Q2 2026? How did producer/output prices, input costs, weather/harvest prospects, livestock/crop differences, exports/ruble, inventories and support affect margins and cash-flow timing? How did preferential lending/subsidy mechanics and seasonal working capital alter credit cost, access, demand and debt-service risk? Which AПК segments diverged?

**Scope.** Agricultural output and prices; crop/livestock conditions; input-cost channels (fuel, fertilizer, labor, machinery/interest) where evidenced; harvest/seasonality; exports and domestic pricing; preferential short/long-term lending and material support; investment/working-capital/debt-service implications.

**Non-goals.** Full agricultural market handbook; farm-level underwriting model; unsupported harvest forecasting.

**Evidence strategy.** Rosstat, Ministry of Agriculture/Government, Bank of Russia regional/enterprise material, official preferential-lending rules/data, credible sector sources for harvest/market conditions and cross-checks.

**Challenges.** Calendar versus agricultural season, regional/weather heterogeneity, crop versus livestock economics, export duty/quota effects, subsidy timing, nominal price movements versus real margins.

**Stopping.** Enough to explain where AПК follows general high-rate/cooling mechanisms and where sector-specific mechanisms dominate or offset them.

### TOPIC-07 — Major-bank IFRS lens on corporate-economic conditions

**Activation.** Added by developed human input `INPUT-0002` after completion of TOPIC-01–06. It is a supplementary evidence lane and does not replace official macro/credit statistics.

**Problem.** IFRS disclosures of large Russian banks can reveal borrower behavior and stress earlier or at a finer portfolio level than aggregate statistics, but each bank is institution-specific. The task is to establish what this evidence adds to the understanding of the Russian corporate economy without confusing bank portfolio effects with economy-wide facts.

**Questions.** What do H1/Q2 2026 IFRS statements and related reporting of major Russian banks show about corporate loan growth/composition, sector concentration, stage migration and NPLs, cost of risk, restructurings/forbearance, collateral or provision dynamics, corporate deposits/liquidity and management-described borrower behavior? Which signals corroborate, qualify or contradict TOPIC-03 and TOPIC-05? Where available, what does agribusiness exposure add to TOPIC-06?

**Scope.** A purposive sample of major Russian banks with sufficiently current H1/Q2 2026 IFRS disclosure and material corporate lending. Primary IFRS financial statements, notes and official IFRS-result presentations/releases are admissible. 2025 comparative data are included where needed for direction or baseline.

**Non-goals.** Ranking banks; assessing bank investment attractiveness; forecasting bank capital/NIM/profit; mechanically aggregating institution-level values into national estimates.

**Evidence strategy.** Prefer audited/reviewed IFRS statements and notes, then official results presentations/releases for management explanations. Capture bank-specific portfolio mix, accounting definitions and risk policy as applicability limits. Seek cross-bank convergence only where signals are semantically comparable and independent enough to support it.

**Challenges.** Selection and portfolio-mix bias; consolidation perimeter; IFRS 9 model effects; FX/revaluation; acquisitions; one-off large borrowers; differing Stage 3/NPL definitions; management overlays; bank-specific de-risking or growth strategy.

**Stopping.** Sufficient when the bank-reporting lane either materially changes/strengthens the corporate-finance or credit interpretation, or is shown to add little beyond aggregate sources, with transfer limits explicit.
